How it works

A card, not an accountPeople carry an ordinary NFC card. Pont only reads its number; the points live on the server, so a card can't be edited to gain points.
Points at every tapA fixed amount at a reader, any amount from a staff phone, or points for the time someone spends inside an area.
Everyone seesThe holder sees their points and history in the app, and a screen beside the reader shows each tap with the person's photo.

Three ways to give points

  • A phone as a point ATM. Type an amount or tap a preset, hold a card to the phone, and the points land. The phone shows the holder's photo so the face can be checked, and any charge can be cancelled.
  • Small ESP32 readers. A box with a card reader and a four-digit display gives a fixed number of points per tap, or works as a sign-in and sign-out point. It joins the server by itself and an admin approves it.
  • Check-in areas. Sign in at a door, sign out when leaving, and the server pays by the area's rules: points per block of minutes, a longest stay, a daily cap.

A screen that shows every tap

Open the display page on any screen, type the reader's six-digit code, and each tap appears as a big, readable result: the photo, the name, what was given and the new balance. A cancel button can be switched on for each reader, for a card tapped by mistake.

The program, laid out by time

For events with many things going on, the program shows every activity as a row against the clock: fixed hours as solid bars, open-for-hours activities as dashed bands, with the room, the points and what to bring. In the app you can drag an activity onto your day, choose how long you will stay and watch the points you could earn add up against the goal.

Run the event from one website

The admin site manages cards with their photos, readers, check-in areas, staff and every transaction. It updates by itself as things happen, imports a whole list of cards in one go, and shows a report for each person: where they were, when, and what each visit paid, on a timeline. The same site is laid out for phones.

Open source, small, and yours

A Node and SQLite server, an admin website, ESP32 reader firmware and Android apps, all in one repository. It runs on a modest machine, keeps its data in one database file, and every balance change is written to a ledger that can be cancelled but not edited.
